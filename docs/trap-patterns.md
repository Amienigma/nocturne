# Trap pattern library: scene notes

These are the working notes behind NOCTURNE's 32-groove **PATTERN LIBRARY**.
Every groove is an original step sequence written for this app. The names
describe a *style*, never an artist or a song. The notes cover the shared
conventions of each scene (tempo, grid, drum placement, 808 behaviour), which
are genre traits, not anyone's recordings.

Grid: 32 steps = 2 bars of 16ths. Rolls use the step ratchet (`rat`): 2 = 1/32,
3 = 1/16 triplet, 4 = 1/64, and so on, plus velocity ramps.

## Atlanta (ATL)

The template most people mean by "trap".

- **Tempo / feel:** 130–150 BPM, written in half time, so the snare or clap
  lands on beat 3 of each bar (step 8 and step 24 of 32). A few grooves sit
  at a true 70–80 BPM with a looser swing.
- **Hats:** the identity of the style. Straight 16ths are broken up with 1/32
  and 1/64 rolls, triplet runs into the snare, and velocity ramps (soft to
  hard). Sparse "skippy" patterns leave deliberate gaps.
- **Kick and 808:** the kick usually doubles the 808 on its first hit. 808s are
  long and tuned, with octave or fifth **glides** (slides) into the next note.
  Syncopated pickups come just before the snare or the bar line.
- **Melody styles reflected in the library:**
  - church keys and organ bounce (gospel-rooted chords)
  - dark orchestral minor loops (strings, bells)
  - minimal dark bells under heavy rolls
  - flutes and plucks
  - spacey, washed pads
  - bouncy melodic plucks with off-kilter kick placement
- **Producer lineages studied:**
  - keys-driven church bounce
  - cinematic dark minor
  - heavy 808 rolls
  - spacey, hazy, off-grid bounce
  - minimal, sparse bells
  - the sharp, syncopated kick-and-808 bounce

  These are styles only; nothing in the library copies a specific beat.

## Memphis (MEM)

The roots of trap's triplet flow and of modern phonk.

- **Tempo / feel:** either about 128–140 BPM and driving, or a crawling
  70–80 BPM ("horrorcore" and gangsta-walk tempo).
- **Drums:** the 808-kit sound (dry kick, snappy clap), a **cowbell** carrying
  a melody, and triplet hat gallops. Rattle-snare fills come before turnarounds.
  Kicks are often pitched to act as the bassline.
- **Mood:** minor and phrygian. Detuned saws, horror-movie strings, lo-fi grit.
  Buck and crunk stomp grooves for the club side.
- **The modern Memphis bounce:** fast-tempo, punchy kick-and-808 bounces with
  space between hits. Represented by "Bounce Knock".

## Houston (HTX)

Slow, heavy and swung.

- **Tempo / feel:** 60–84 BPM with heavy swing (6–18 % in the library).
  "Screwed" means pitched down and slowed. "Chopped" means stutter-repeats
  and skips. The library uses stutter steps and repeated hits to suggest the
  chop.
- **Drums:** laid-back snare and clap on 2 and 4, open-hat lifts, and fewer
  rolls. The space *is* the groove.
- **808 / bass:** long sliding 808s and walking funk bass lines (slab and
  candy-paint cruise feel). G-funk-style lead synths and organ stabs. Dorian
  and minor colours.
- **Scene traits:** the slab bounce, the Southside and Northside knock, and the
  syrupy "purple" drag.

## New wave (NEW)

Modern offshoots that grew out of the three scenes.

- **Rage:** 150–165 BPM, distorted 808s, loud supersaw-style leads, and
  stomping kicks on every beat or every half beat.
- **Drill-adjacent:** 140-ish BPM with sliding 808s that jump intervals,
  triplet hat cells, and a snare on beat 4 (step 24 of 32).
- **Plugg / pluggnb:** 145–150 BPM, airy bells and soft chords, gentle 808
  glides, and sparse soft hats.
- **Hyper trap:** 160+ BPM with constant 1/32 rolls.

Each groove sets the following for the **section you're editing only** (one
undo step):

- drums, including rolls and ramps
- the 808 line, including glides
- a synth part
- tempo and swing
